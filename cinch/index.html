Privacy Policy

Cinch compresses videos entirely on your iPhone or iPad, using the media frameworks built into iOS. Your videos never leave your device.

  • No data collection. We do not collect personal information, usage data, analytics, or any identifiers.
  • No accounts. There's nothing to sign up for.
  • No uploads. Every compression happens on-device. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • No third-party tracking. No SDKs, no telemetry, no ads.
  • No network requests. Cinch makes no network connections and works fully offline.

Your videos stay on your device. We never see them.

Photo Library Access

Cinch asks for permission to add videos to your photo library so it can save the compressed copy back. It reads a video only when you choose it in the picker, share it into Cinch, or import it from Files. It cannot browse your library on its own.

Local Storage

Cinch stores a small amount of data locally on your device:

  • Your chosen target size, format, and audio setting
  • How many free compressions you have used
  • A list of recent compressions — file names and before/after sizes only

This data never leaves your device and is never transmitted anywhere. The compressed videos themselves are not stored by Cinch; they are saved where you choose and working copies are deleted automatically.

Purchases

The one-time unlock is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. We do not have access to your payment information. Apple's privacy policy governs these transactions.

Children's Privacy

Cinch does not collect any data from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to This Policy

If we ever change this policy, we will update the date above. Given that we collect no data, we do not anticipate significant changes.

Common Questions

Where do my compressed videos go?
Nowhere until you say so. Tap Save to Photos to put them in your photo library, or Share to send them straight to another app. Working copies are cleared automatically.

Cinch says my video won't fit. What now?
Some videos are simply too long to reach a small target at watchable quality. Tap Trim to fit and Cinch works out the longest stretch that will fit, then lets you choose which part to keep. Removing the audio also frees up room for the picture.

What's the difference between H.264 and HEVC?
HEVC fits noticeably more detail into the same file size, but not everything can play it — some Windows PCs and older Android phones struggle. H.264 is the default because it plays essentially anywhere. If the recipient is on a recent iPhone, HEVC is the better choice.

Why does the quality drop so much?
A file size limit is a fixed budget. A long video sharing that budget across many minutes has less to spend on each one. Choose a larger target, trim the video, or remove the audio.

Can I compress several videos at once?
Yes. Choose up to 25 from your photo library, or share up to 10 into Cinch at once. They are compressed one at a time so your device doesn't run out of memory.

What does the share extension do?
It lets you compress straight from Photos or any other app without opening Cinch. Select a video, tap Share, then tap Cinch.

Can I use Cinch in Shortcuts?
Yes. The Compress Video action takes a video and a target size, and can be used in automations.

What's in the "Recent" list?
Only file names and before/after sizes. The compressed videos themselves are not kept — storing copies of everything you ever compressed would quietly fill your device, which is the opposite of what Cinch is for.

How many free compressions do I get?
Three. After that, a single one-time purchase unlocks unlimited compressions. It is not a subscription.
